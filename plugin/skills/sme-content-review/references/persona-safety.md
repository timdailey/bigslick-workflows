# Persona-safety pass

The author's `Voice:` section names who reads them. Read the draft as each of those people in turn, and flag anything that could offend, alienate or backfire. This doesn't mute the author: don't flag sharpness itself, and a contrarian view backed by substance is a strength. The job is to catch the specific lines that cross into offensive, bitter or self-sabotaging, and fix them without losing the edge.

Rate each flag with the review's severities:

- **Blocker:** don't publish as written. A clear reputational or legal risk.
- **Major:** defensible, but it has a real downside, so the author should make the call.
- **Minor:** easy polish.

## Questions for common audiences

Use whichever match the section. For another audience, ask what would make that reader trust the author less.

**Buyer.** Does this make the author look like someone to buy from?
- Contempt for the reader's current way of working, or for their peers.
- Arrogance without substance.
- A promise the company can't keep.

**Investor.** Is anything careless, overclaimed or legally exposed?
- Unverifiable specifics, or inflated numbers stated as fact.
- Absolutes such as "always", "never" and "guaranteed".
- Trashing a named competitor or company. Critique the idea, not the target.
- Confidential detail: client names, deal terms, internal metrics or roadmap.

**Candidate.** Would this make a good person want to work here?
- Airing internal problems, or blaming a team.
- Bitterness about a former employer or colleague.
- Cruelty or meanness, even as a joke.

## For every audience

- Stereotyping or punching down at any group.
- Mocking a named person.
- A punchline built on someone's identity rather than an idea.
- A politically or culturally charged aside that isn't the substance of the piece.

If the draft is clean for an audience, say so briefly. Don't invent problems.
