# Humanize pass

Read the draft for signs that it was written by a machine. Check it against the author's `Voice:` section first: a word or habit that section shows is theirs is not a tell, and neither is a normal term of the client's field.

## Vocabulary

These rarely appear in how people actually talk: delve, leverage (as a verb), robust, seamless, landscape, realm, tapestry, underscore, pivotal, testament, boasts, elevate, unlock, harness, navigate (figurative), spearhead, foster, myriad, plethora, crucial, vital, game-changer, revolutionize, cutting-edge, empower, resonate, curate, bespoke, holistic, synergy, paradigm, ecosystem (when vague), streamline, supercharge, turnkey, best-in-class, next-level, transformative.

## Scaffolding and clichés

- "It is not just X, it is Y", and "not only... but also".
- "In today's fast-paced world", "in an era of" or "in the age of AI" as an opener.
- "Let's dive in", "let's unpack this", "buckle up".
- "The truth is", "here is the thing", "make no mistake".
- "At the end of the day", "when all is said and done".
- "Imagine a world where", "this begs the question".
- Tidy closers: "in conclusion", "ultimately", "the bottom line is".
- Hedges that blunt a point: "arguably", "it is worth noting", "that said".

## Rhythm and structure

- Every sentence about the same length.
- Everything in threes.
- Balanced parallel phrasing in every paragraph.
- Every paragraph the same shape.
- A rhetorical "X? Y." setup more than once in a piece.

## Surface

- Emoji and hashtag stacks the author doesn't use.
- Em dashes and ampersands, if `Style rules` bans them.
- Title Case section headers inside a short post.

## The genericness test

This is the biggest tell. A draft with no concrete number, name, lived detail or real opinion reads as machine-written however clean the prose is. It needs at least one line that only this author would write, and a false detail doesn't count. Say what kind of detail is missing. Never invent the detail, because a made-up specific is a false claim.
